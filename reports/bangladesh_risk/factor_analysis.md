# Exploratory Factor Association Analysis

## Outcome and method

The post-hoc outcome is **Elevated dropout intention**, defined as responses 4–5 versus 1–3 on the survey's 1–5 dropout-intention question. For each candidate factor, a contingency-table chi-square test and Cramér's V were calculated. Ordered factors also received a Spearman correlation with the original 1–5 response.

These are unadjusted, exploratory, bivariate associations. They do not establish causality, do not control for confounding, and should not be treated as a predictive model. Twenty comparisons increase the chance of false positives; small categories can produce unstable percentages.

## Most notable associations

| Factor | Cramér's V | Chi-square p | Interpretation and caution |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Study-material satisfaction | 0.185 | 0.017 | Largest observed effect, but not monotonic (Spearman ρ≈0, p=0.994); rating 5 has only 10 respondents. Do not interpret higher satisfaction as harmful. |
| Scholarship | 0.171 | 0.016 | Private/donor recipients show 40.0% elevated intention versus 17.2% among students without scholarships; category size and selection effects require caution. |
| Study-space quality | 0.170 | 0.070 | Very noisy spaces show 35.5% versus 13.7% for quiet spaces, but the overall test is not significant and the ordinal trend is weak. |
| Employment type | 0.169 | 0.018 | Freelancers show 35.1% versus 13.7% for students not working; work type may proxy financial or time pressure. |
| Prior residence | 0.167 | 0.008 | Rural-origin students show 32.3% versus 16.9% for urban-origin students. This is association, not an inherent-risk claim. |
| Sleep duration | 0.153 | 0.041 | More than 8 hours shows 44.4%, but that category contains only 18 students; the pattern is not safely causal or strictly monotonic. |
| Personal income | 0.142 | 0.069 | Above BDT 10,000 shows 29.3% versus 16.1% for no income; overall evidence is weak and likely intertwined with employment. |
| Resource access | 0.125 | 0.140 | Weekly access shows 27.6% versus 15.6% for daily access; not statistically significant. |
| Gender | 0.122 | 0.031 | Female respondents show 27.9% versus 17.7% for male respondents; this should inform equity-aware support, never discriminatory treatment. |
| Living arrangement | 0.107 | 0.062 | University-hall residents show 28.2% versus 18.8% at home; evidence is weak. |

Attendance has a weak negative ordinal relationship with intention (Spearman ρ=-0.106, p=0.047) even though its categorical chi-square association is not significant (p=0.144). Meal skipping and other remaining factors are weaker in this sample.

## Responsible conclusion

No factor is strong enough to support an individual prediction or intervention by itself. The results are best used to formulate questions for a broader, representative longitudinal study. If displayed, the website should label all percentages as sample-level context, show denominators, avoid causal language, and warn when a category is small.
